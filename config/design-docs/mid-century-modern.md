# Mid-Century Modern

## What it is

Mid-century modern (MCM) is a postwar design language: clean organic lines, honest materials, and furniture that looks light on the floor. Think teak and walnut case goods, tapered legs, molded plywood or fiberglass seats, and a mix of natural wood with brass, leather, or stone. It is not vintage clutter — it is edited, purposeful, and warm without heavy ornament.

## Do

- Prioritize tapered legs and visible structure on sofas, chairs, and tables so the room feels open.
- Use walnut, teak, or oak as the wood story; keep finishes consistent within a room (one dominant wood tone).
- Anchor with a low sofa or sectional, a credenza or media cabinet, and one sculptural light (globe, sputnik, or arched floor lamp).
- Mix organic curves (egg-chair lineage, kidney coffee tables) with rectangular case goods for balance.
- Keep walls quiet (off-white, warm gray, soft olive) so wood and one accent color carry personality.
- Scale rugs large enough to catch front legs of seating; MCM rooms fail when rugs float too small.

## Don't

- Do not pile on Victorian carving, skirted sofas, or heavy drapery — they fight the lightness.
- Avoid chrome-everything futuristic kitsch unless you intentionally want Space Age as an accent only.
- Do not mix five wood tones; two is the max (primary + one accent wood).
- Skip busy floral wallpapers as the default; if pattern appears, keep it geometric and limited.

## Pairings and ratios

- Palette: 60% warm neutrals / 30% wood + textile / 10% mustard, teal, or terracotta.
- Metals: brass or blackened steel — pick one as dominant; a second metal only on small hardware.
- Soft goods: wool, boucle, or leather in solid or subtle geometric weave; avoid shabby florals.
- Lighting layers: ambient pendant or flush + reading floor lamp + low cabinet light; MCM loves a statement fixture but still needs task light.

## Room recipes

Living: low sofa, walnut coffee table, credenza, arched lamp, large wool rug. Dining: round or oval table with tapered chairs; one sideboard. Bedroom: platform or simple headboard, nightstands with tapered legs, warm wood dresser.

## Budget tradeoffs

Spend on the sofa and one wood case piece first; lamps and side tables can be phased. A single authentic-feeling wood finish beats three cheap mismatched MCM-ish pieces.

## Conflict watch

MCM + farmhouse: keep wood warm but drop barn doors and shiplap as the main story. MCM + industrial: allow metal legs and concrete, but keep wood volume higher than raw steel.

## Detail checklist for shopping

When evaluating a sofa, look for a low back, clean arm, and legs that taper or are clearly separated from the skirt. For tables, prefer visible joinery or a simple pedestal over chunky block bases. Case goods should sit lightly: a credenza with tapered legs will open the floor visually more than a full plinth. Soft goods work best in solids or small geometrics — think broken stripe or subtle diamond, not floral chintz. If you already own a heavy traditional piece, keep it only if you can strip visual weight (remove skirt, simplify hardware) or demote it to another room.

## Sequence for a full living room

1. Confirm wood tone and metal. 2. Place sofa and rug together so the rug is large enough. 3. Add the primary wood case piece (credenza or bookcase). 4. Add coffee table and one accent chair. 5. Finish with the sculptural light and two art moments. This order prevents buying lamps and pillows before the silhouette story is locked. If budget is tight, stop after step 3 and live with temporary side tables rather than cheaping out on the sofa.

## EXPLAIN anchors Eva can cite

Tapered legs, walnut/teak story, 60/30/10 with mustard or teal, brass-or-steel metal discipline, and "light on the floor" as the spatial test. When the user says mid-century but also wants farmhouse, name the tension and propose MCM dominant with one rustic accent only.
