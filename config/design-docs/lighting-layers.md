# Lighting Layers

## Three layers every room needs

1. Ambient — overall glow (ceiling flush, pendant, recessed on dimmers, or reflected uplight).
2. Task — reading, cooking, desk, makeup (floor lamp, under-cabinet, swing-arm, vanity).
3. Accent — art wash, shelf light, candle-level glow that adds depth at night.

A single bright ceiling fixture is not a lighting plan. Layers let you shift from cleaning mode to evening mode without changing the furniture.

## Practical rules

- Prefer warm white (2700–3000K) in living and bedrooms; cooler task light only where detail work needs it.
- Put ambient and key lamps on dimmers whenever possible.
- Height: pendants over tables leave clearance for heads and sightlines; dining pendants typically hang so the bottom sits roughly 30–36 inches above the table (adjust for ceiling height).
- Floor lamps work beside sofas when tables are scarce; avoid blocking walkways.
- Avoid only downlights; bounce light off walls/ceilings for softness.

## Style cues

- MCM: sculptural floor or sputnik + soft ambient.
- Scandi/Japandi: paper, linen, or frosted glass; gentle pools of light.
- Industrial: cage/track plus warm dimming — not harsh warehouse glare.
- Traditional: chandelier or lantern ambient + paired table lamps.
- Coastal: simple pendants, linen shades, daylight first.

## Do / Don't

Do light dark corners and pathways. Do match lamp scale to furniture. Don't rely on overhead alone for living rooms. Don't mix five unrelated fixture styles; pick a family and vary size.

## Room-by-room minimums

Living: ambient + two lamps + optional accent. Dining: dimmable pendant + sideboard lamp or wall lights. Bedroom: ambient soft + two bedside tasks + optional closet/task. Home office: ambient + desk task that does not bounce glare on screens. Kitchen: ambient + under-cabinet task + pendant over island if present.

## Glare, shadows, and safety

Avoid bare bulbs at eye level in conversation zones. Light stairs and hall changes of level. In kids' rooms, prioritize covered fixtures and warm enough light for wind-down. If recessed cans are the only option, add lamps anyway — cans alone flatten faces and furniture.

## Bulbs and control

Match color temperature within a room. Use dimmers on ambient circuits. Smart bulbs help only if the user will actually set scenes; otherwise a dimmer switch is more reliable. Measure brightness by activity, not by maximum wattage bragging.

## EXPLAIN anchors

Ambient/task/accent triad, 2700–3000K living spaces, dimmers, pendant clearance over tables, do not rely on overhead alone, style-specific fixture families.

## Field note

Re-check the room after each major purchase: does the latest piece reinforce the documented direction, or does it reopen a style conflict? If it reopens conflict, name the tension and re-assert dominant versus accent before buying further. Prefer one decisive correction over three small compromises that blur the plan. Keep shopping lists short: core pieces first, accents last, and only after the readiness of the room plan justifies them.
