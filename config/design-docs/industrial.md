# Industrial

## What it is

Industrial interiors borrow from factories and lofts: exposed structure, metal, concrete, brick, utilitarian lighting, and a slightly unfinished honesty. Done well it feels strong and edited — not like a theme restaurant.

## Do

- Show structure when you have it (brick, beams, ductwork) or suggest it with materials (metal frames, concrete-look surfaces).
- Use metal + wood pairings: steel table bases with wood tops, pipe shelving with wood shelves.
- Lighting: cage pendants, track, warm bulbs — dimmable and layered, not only novelty bulbs.
- Palette: charcoal, black, warm gray, rust, brown, white. Leather and canvas for soft goods.
- Keep layouts open; industrial likes long sightlines and sturdy pathways.

## Don't

- Do not cover every wall in faux brick; one honest material moment beats wallpaper everywhere.
- Avoid pastel shabby finishes and ornate traditional furniture as the main story.
- Do not leave the room cold and echoey — add textiles (rugs, leather, canvas) so it is livable.
- Skip mixing five metal finishes; pick iron/black steel as dominant.

## Pairings and ratios

- 50% hard materials (metal, concrete, brick) / 30% wood / 20% soft textile.
- Contrast: matte black metal against warm wood prevents a sterile look.
- Art: large-scale photography or abstract works; avoid tiny cluttered gallery walls unless intentional.

## Room recipes

Living: leather or canvas sofa, metal coffee table, open shelving, oversized rug. Dining: wood-and-steel table, mixed metal chairs, pendant over center. Office: metal desk frame, wood top, task lamp, cork or pegboard utility wall.

## Budget tradeoffs

Prioritize a strong table or sofa and good lighting. Open shelving is cheaper than custom cabinetry but demands editing. Rugs and curtains soften acoustics without hiding the industrial bones.

## Conflict watch

Industrial + traditional: keep wood traditional pieces but strip ornate fabrics; let metal lighting bridge. Industrial + Scandi: raise wood and soft textile ratios so the room does not feel like a warehouse.

## Softening without losing the bones

Industrial rooms that people actually love almost always include a large rug, curtains or soft window treatment, and at least one upholstered seat. Acoustics matter in hard lofts — textiles are functional, not a betrayal of the style. If brick is fake and shiny, reduce how much of it you show and compensate with real wood and honest metal. Keep pathways wide; industrial furniture is often heavier visually and needs clearance.

## Kitchen and work zones

Open metal shelves work when dishes are edited to one palette. Track lighting helps task zones; warm it down at night. For desks, a wood top on a metal base is the classic bridge. Avoid treating every wall as a utility board — one pegboard or rail is enough.

## EXPLAIN anchors

Metal + wood pairing, brick/concrete honesty, warm dimmable utilitarian light, leather/canvas soft goods, open sightlines. Soften warehouses with textile ratio before adding more steel.

## Field note

Re-check the room after each major purchase: does the latest piece reinforce the documented direction, or does it reopen a style conflict? If it reopens conflict, name the tension and re-assert dominant versus accent before buying further. Prefer one decisive correction over three small compromises that blur the plan. Keep shopping lists short: core pieces first, accents last, and only after the readiness of the room plan justifies them.
