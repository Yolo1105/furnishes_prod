# Coastal

## What it is

Coastal interiors evoke light, air, and shore without becoming a beach souvenir shop. Soft blues and sandy neutrals, weathered or light woods, easy textiles, and a breezy layout. It should feel relaxed and sunlit — not themed with anchors on every pillow.

## Do

- Palette: sand, white, soft blue, seafoam, driftwood gray; keep saturation gentle.
- Materials: light wood, rattan/wicker accents, linen, cotton, jute or sisal rugs.
- Maximize breezy light: sheer curtains, reflective whites, uncluttered window walls.
- Furniture: slipcovered or simple sofas, light tables, open woven accents.
- Bring nature in with organic shapes and a few shells/stone objects — edited, not crowded.
- Layer soft textiles for comfort without heavy formal drapery.

## Don't

- Do not overdo nautical motifs (anchors, ropes, ship wheels) as the primary decoration.
- Avoid dark heavy traditional furniture as the main story unless transitional coastal is intentional.
- Do not use only cold blue-gray with no warm sand — rooms go sterile.
- Skip high-gloss yacht finishes everywhere; matte and washed woods feel more livable.

## Pairings and ratios

- 60% sand/white / 30% soft blue-green textile / 10% darker navy or natural wood accent.
- Metals: brushed nickel or soft brass in small doses; avoid industrial black as dominant.
- Patterns: stripes, soft geometrics, or subtle botanical — one pattern family per room.

## Room recipes

Living: slipcovered sofa, jute rug, light wood coffee table, woven side chairs, sheer curtains. Dining: washed wood table, mixed light chairs, linen runner, simple pendant. Bedroom: white bedding, soft blue throw, rattan accent, pale dresser.

## Budget tradeoffs

Invest in sofa and window sheers; coastal depends on light and softness. Rattan accents and jute rugs stretch the look affordably. Art can be photography of sky/water without literal beach kitsch.

## Conflict watch

Coastal + industrial: keep palette soft; confine metal to lighting. Coastal + traditional: allow classic sofa shape but keep fabrics light and patterns quiet.

## Climate and material honesty

Coastal style should respect humidity and sand: washable slipcovers, durable natural fibers, finishes that can take air and light. Avoid delicate silks as the main upholstery story. If the home is inland, borrow coastal through palette and light woods rather than literal seashell displays. Weathered wood is a finish story, not an excuse for damaged furniture.

## Outdoor adjacency

When rooms open to a porch or patio, repeat the sand/blue thread outside with cushions and one shared metal finish so the transition feels continuous. Keep indoor rugs durable near entries. Sheer indoor curtains that move in breeze reinforce the coastal idea more than theme decor.

## EXPLAIN anchors

Sand and soft blue palette, light wood and rattan, linen/cotton/jute, sheer daylight, edited natural objects, no nautical kitsch. Mix with traditional by lightening fabrics, not by adding anchors.

## Field note

Re-check the room after each major purchase: does the latest piece reinforce the documented direction, or does it reopen a style conflict? If it reopens conflict, name the tension and re-assert dominant versus accent before buying further. Prefer one decisive correction over three small compromises that blur the plan. Keep shopping lists short: core pieces first, accents last, and only after the readiness of the room plan justifies them.
