# Color Theory and Palettes

## Color relationships that work in rooms

- Complementary (opposites, e.g. blue/orange): high energy — use one as dominant, the other as 10% accent.
- Analogous (neighbors, e.g. blue/blue-green/green): easy harmony for walls and large upholstery.
- Triadic: vibrant; assign 60/30/10 so one hue leads.
- Warm palettes (red/orange/yellow families) feel cozy and social; cool (blue/green/violet) feel calmer and more spacious.

## The 60-30-10 rule

Use 60% dominant (walls, large sofa), 30% secondary (curtains, chairs, rug field), 10% accent (pillows, art, lamps). Apply by room: living room warm gray walls + sofa (60), navy curtains and chair (30), mustard pillows (10). Bedroom: walls + bedding (60), headboard + rug (30), lamps and art (10).

## Named starting palettes

- Warm neutral: beige or greige walls, soft white trim, navy or camel accent.
- Cool modern: alabaster walls, charcoal furniture, brass hardware accents.
- Calm retreat: sage or dusty blue walls, cream upholstery, wood + greenery as the 10%.
- Coastal sand: sand walls, soft blue textiles, white trim, natural fiber rug.
- MCM punch: warm white walls, walnut wood, mustard or teal 10%.

## Perceived size

Light cool colors expand; dark warm colors cozy and shrink. Ceilings: lighter than walls raises height; darker ceilings lower it (use carefully in tall rooms). Consistent trim color helps rooms feel larger.

## Where to place bold color

Low commitment: pillows, art, lamps. Medium: rugs, upholstery chairs. High: walls and large sofas. Start bold in the 10% and expand only if you love living with it.

## Do / Don't

Do repeat each color at least twice in the room so accents feel intentional. Do test paint in morning and evening light. Don't introduce a fifth unrelated hue. Don't ignore undertones (yellow-beige vs pink-beige clash next to cool gray).

## Undertones and adjacency

Beiges with yellow undertones fight cool gray walls. Navy with purple undertones can clash with green-based sage. Always check samples next to existing sofa fabric and flooring, not only on a white counter. In open plans, carry the 60% dominant across zones and vary only the 10% accents per area so the home still coheres.

## Neutrals that earn their keep

A good neutral is not "absence of color"; it has a temperature. Warm greige supports walnut and brass. Cool greige supports chrome and charcoal. White trim should be chosen deliberately (bright white vs soft white) and repeated. Black used as a thin outline (frames, lamps) can sharpen a calm palette without becoming a fourth color family.

## Testing method

Paint sample boards, move them around for 48 hours, view at night with your actual lamps. Fabric swatches should sit on the sofa in daylight and lamplight. If the 10% accent feels loud after a week, reduce its surface area before abandoning the whole palette.

## EXPLAIN anchors Eva should use

60-30-10, warm vs cool, complementary accents in small doses, named palettes (warm neutral, cool modern, calm retreat), and undertone matching to existing wood and textile.

## Field note

Re-check the room after each major purchase: does the latest piece reinforce the documented direction, or does it reopen a style conflict? If it reopens conflict, name the tension and re-assert dominant versus accent before buying further. Prefer one decisive correction over three small compromises that blur the plan. Keep shopping lists short: core pieces first, accents last, and only after the readiness of the room plan justifies them.
