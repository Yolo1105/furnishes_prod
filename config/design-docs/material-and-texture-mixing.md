# Material and Texture Mixing

## Why texture matters

Even a neutral palette fails if every surface is flat and hard. Mixing materials creates depth: wood grain, soft textile, matte ceramic, a bit of metal or stone. The goal is contrast you can feel, not clutter you can count.

## Reliable pairings

- Wood + linen + wool: calm residential default (Scandi/Japandi/MCM).
- Wood + leather + metal: stronger, more tailored or industrial.
- Stone/concrete + wood + soft rug: hard architecture softened for living.
- Rattan + linen + light wood: coastal/casual without kitsch.
- Velvet + wood + brass: traditional or glam accent — keep velvet limited if the rest is casual.

## Ratios

Aim for roughly 40% soft (upholstery, rug, curtains) / 40% natural hard (wood, stone) / 20% reflective or cool (metal, glass). Adjust per style: industrial raises hard; Scandi raises soft.

## Do

- Repeat each material at least twice (two wood tones of the same family, two metal moments).
- Vary sheen: matte walls, soft fabric, one polished accent (lamp, tray).
- Use rug texture to define zones in open plans.
- Match metal finishes within a room unless you intentionally mix two (e.g. black + brass) and repeat both.

## Don't

- Do not mix five woods with different undertones.
- Avoid all-shiny surfaces (gloss cabinets + glass + chrome) — rooms feel cold and show fingerprints.
- Do not stack every texture on one sofa (pattern + boucle + leather pillows + knit) without solids nearby.
- Skip fake materials that fight the story (high-gloss plastic "wood" next to real oak).

## Room checks

If the room photographs flat, add one tactile layer (throw, woven basket, ceramic lamp). If it feels busy, remove one pattern or one metal finish first before buying more.

## Soft vs hard balance test

Stand in the doorway: if you mostly see hard shiny planes, add rug and textile. If you mostly see busy fabric, add a calm wood or stone plane. Kitchens need this test too — wood stools and a runner often fix showroom coldness. Bathrooms benefit from one textile (towel set + bathmat) in the palette story so they join the home.

## Mixing eras without mud

A contemporary sofa can live with an antique wood chest if metals are disciplined and the palette is shared. The failure mode is mixing eras and materials and colors all at once. Change one axis at a time. When in doubt, reduce material count to three: wood, cloth, metal.

## Care and longevity

High-touch surfaces should be honest about maintenance: matte black metal shows dust; linen wrinkles; marble etches. Tell users the care tradeoff when recommending materials so EXPLAIN includes lived reality, not only aesthetics.

## EXPLAIN anchors

40/40/20 soft-hard-reflective starting point, repeat materials twice, limit wood undertones, limit metal finishes, add tactile layer when photos look flat.

## Field note

Re-check the room after each major purchase: does the latest piece reinforce the documented direction, or does it reopen a style conflict? If it reopens conflict, name the tension and re-assert dominant versus accent before buying further. Prefer one decisive correction over three small compromises that blur the plan. Keep shopping lists short: core pieces first, accents last, and only after the readiness of the room plan justifies them.
