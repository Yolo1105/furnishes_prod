# Small-Space Strategies

Numeric walkway clearances and rug-sizing standards are owned by Eva's
deterministic design-rule tables (`design-rules.ts`). Prefer those over any
prose numbers in this doc when they disagree.

## Priorities

In tight rooms, circulation and scale beat decoration. Measure walkways, choose fewer larger pieces over many small ones, and use vertical storage. The room should feel intentional, not stuffed.

## Layout moves

- Keep 30–36 inches for main walkways where possible; protect the primary path first.
- Float a sofa only if clearance remains; otherwise place on the longest wall.
- Use rugs that underlap front legs to enlarge the perceived floor plane.
- Prefer legs you can see under furniture; solid skirted bases eat visual space.
- Multifunction: storage ottoman, nesting tables, wall-mounted shelves, sofa beds only when truly needed.

## Visual tricks

- Light walls and continuous floor color expand.
- Mirrors opposite or adjacent to windows bounce light — not mirrored walls as default.
- One tall element (bookshelf, plant, curtain to ceiling) draws the eye up.
- Curtains mounted high and wide make windows feel larger.
- Limit palette to 2–3 hues so the eye rests.

## Do / Don't

Do edit surfaces weekly; clutter shrinks rooms fastest. Do choose a sofa depth that fits with a coffee table and walkway. Don't buy a sectional that fills the entire footprint unless it is the only seating plan. Don't use multiple tiny rugs that chop the floor into islands.

## Budget

Spend on the sofa (or bed) and good lighting; they dominate small rooms. Skip extra accent chairs until the path is clear. Vertical storage is usually cheaper than a larger apartment.

## Measuring before buying

Record wall lengths, door swings, and radiator or vent clearances. Tape the sofa footprint on the floor with painter's tape for a day. Check elevator and stair turns for delivery. A "perfect" sofa that cannot enter the building is not a recommendation.

## Dual-purpose rooms

Studio living: sleeping niche with curtains or a low bookcase divider; dining that folds or nests; office that closes away. Keep the public zone (entry sightline) tidier than the private zone. Use one rug to define the living conversation area so the bed does not visually own the whole room.

## Vertical and hidden storage

Floor-to-ceiling shelves in one bay beat scattered book piles. Beds with drawers help only if the room still allows clearance to open them. Wall hooks and narrow consoles beat deep cabinets that steal walkway inches.

## EXPLAIN anchors

Protect walkways first, fewer larger pieces, visible legs, high-mounted curtains, limited palette, edit clutter, measure delivery paths.

## Field note

Re-check the room after each major purchase: does the latest piece reinforce the documented direction, or does it reopen a style conflict? If it reopens conflict, name the tension and re-assert dominant versus accent before buying further. Prefer one decisive correction over three small compromises that blur the plan. Keep shopping lists short: core pieces first, accents last, and only after the readiness of the room plan justifies them.
